# HeatCore technology viewer — MVP map

Source: `design/references/technology_frame.PNG` (954 × 1649) supplies the reusable black margins, metallic bevel, thin keylines, top `Swipe for Details` with gold right arrow, and bottom `Next Technology` with gold down arrow. Its five static dots were a product defect for this four-image group. The current user correction requires four interactive dots and makes both labeled frame regions actions. The TouchLink photo inside the opening is replaced by image-only HeatCore content; the top-right X closes the viewer. The first group is `design/references/tech_01/`: four clean 941 × 1672 RGB PNGs numbered 02–05. The four `*_TEXT` images are excluded. No group after HeatCore is present.

Correction target: retain the source pixels outside its central opening x83–870/y186–1465 and approved dot band x382–572/y110–140; preserve the outer bevel at x57–897/y159–1490. Create one source-derived RGBA frame with transparent interior and stack it over a variable artwork layer. The five source dots are erased from the band; native source-derived active and outline dot sprites represent exactly four slides with 44px button targets. At Pro 402×874/DPR3, map source X/Y to the full viewer in normalized coordinates because the template reaches the screen edges; compare masked pixels outside the opening and revised band. Pro Max 440×956 is a responsive check. Desktop keeps a centered portrait canvas against black.

Mapping: `technology-experience` at source Y6810 and `technology-repeat` at Y13182 both open `#technology-viewer`. The gallery maps 02 → Three Heaters, 03 → Active Air Sails, 04 → Programmable Heat Profiles, 05 → Gold and Titanium Nitride. Tapping `Swipe for Details`, horizontal touch/pointer swipe and Arrow Left/Right advance images cyclically. Each of the four dots selects its image; the gold filled dot tracks the last decoded, visible image even during delayed/failed loads. Pointer capture retains a gesture that ends beyond the artwork edge; Arabic reverses the swipe direction with its RTL keyboard mapping. Tapping `Next Technology` or swiping down shows a localized, accessible “coming soon” placeholder without changing groups. Upward movement at the first group has no destination. Native `dialog` provides modality and Escape, and X restores trigger focus. Screen-reader instructions, button names, placeholder and slide alt descriptions are locale-keyed in `TechnologyViewer.tsx`; visible frame lettering stays pixel-identical to the English source.

`tools/generate-technology-frame.py` punches the source opening x83–870/y186–1465 inclusive and paints the dot band x382–572/y110–140 black, retaining byte-exact decoded RGB and opaque alpha at every pixel outside these two regions. It also extracts exact source pixels for `dot-active.png` and `dot-inactive.png`; no gold approximation is used. The stage geometry uses source coordinates as percentages of the 954 × 1649 canvas; the frame raster scales to the mobile viewport in both axes. Thus fidelity means the original source is scaled with the viewport, not that its physical aspect ratio is preserved on every phone. The 941 × 1672 slide scales to fill the opening so title and product edges remain visible. Desktop centers a portrait canvas against black. The [tracked WebKit captures](evidence/technology-viewer/checks.md) include Pro, Pro Max, active last dot and next-group placeholder; the complete local set remains under `screenshots/actual/technology-viewer/`. The TouchLink image in the source prevents meaningful full-image comparison; the comparison target is the unchanged frame outside its opening and revised dot band.

The untagged source PNGs have no ICC profile; an intended Photoshop color space is not established. The four web derivatives are 941 × 1672 lossy WebP encoded directly from those sources with `cwebp -q 95 -m 6 -sharp_yuv`, without channel adjustments. They total 807,398 bytes versus roughly 20 MB for four source PNGs. [Asset metrics](evidence/technology-viewer/assets.json) record full source hashes, ICC absence, a lossless candidate (1.21–1.36 MB per image), and q95 decoded error (mean 1.04–1.13 RGB/255, p99 channel error 4–5/255, worst 256px block mean 1.42–1.99/255). The viewer loads its first artwork only when opened, uses tiny inline previews, and retains the previous full image until the next one decodes. The [final local production Chromium sample](evidence/technology-viewer/performance-controls.json) at 402×874/DPR3 measured LCP 96ms, CLS 0, JS transfer 162,381 bytes, zero technology requests before opening and 175ms from opener click to the first decoded photo/frame; two dot sprites together transferred 3,245 bytes. These are synthetic loopback measurements, not field INP or Lighthouse.
